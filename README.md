# 🎯 Action Mapping in Practice

> **Focus on what people need to do—not simply what they need to know.**

As an **Instructional Designer**, I look beyond **knowledge and performance gaps** to understand **what people need to accomplish, what is preventing them from doing it, and how they prefer to learn**.

**Action Mapping** helps me take a **consultative approach** rather than simply responding to client requests. I talk with clients, ask questions to understand **what they need to do and what is getting in the way**, identify the **real cause of the problem**, and design **hands-on activities that meet their needs without overwhelming them with unnecessary information**.

## 🔄 Action Mapping Process

**Interview → Identify Actions → Find the Real Causes → Design Activities → Provide the Solution**

The real cause may be a **skills gap, motivation, environment, or another barrier**. The goal is to understand the problem before deciding that training is the solution.

## 🎯 Example: Learning by Doing

While working as a **Postdoctoral Research Scholar**, I received emails from **junior researchers** asking for help learning how to conduct **meta-analyses in R**.

I interviewed them to understand **what they needed to do, what was preventing them from doing it, and how they preferred to learn**. Through these conversations, I learned that they preferred **learning by doing** when learning how to conduct meta-analyses in R, rather than beginning with a lecture.

We scheduled a session, and I asked them to **install R and RStudio before the session** so we could go directly into practice.

During the session, we opened **RStudio and worked together live**. I demonstrated how to write and understand R code and introduced coding best practices, such as **writing comments as questions**. We worked through the procedures together, solved problems as they came up, and then I asked them to **perform the procedures independently** while I provided feedback.

I also gave them an opportunity to demonstrate whether they could **apply the procedures independently**. Once they were working on their own, they could return to me when they encountered a problem or wanted feedback.

### 🔍 How Action Mapping Shaped the Solution

- **Interview:** Understand what researchers need to accomplish, what is preventing them from doing it, and how they prefer to learn.
- **Actions:** Identify the procedures researchers need to perform independently.
- **Real Causes:** Identify what prevents them from successfully performing those actions.
- **Activities:** Practice the procedures together before moving to independent practice.
- **Feedback:** Provide guidance while researchers work through problems.
- **Solution:** Create on-demand instructional videos that support the actions learners need to perform.

> **Action Mapping helped me move from being an “order taker” to a performance consultant—focusing on what researchers needed to do, identifying the real problem, and designing activities around the actions they needed to perform.**
