# 🎯 Action Mapping in Practice

> **Focus on what people need to do—not simply what they need to know.**

As an **Instructional Designer**, I look beyond **knowledge and performance gaps** to understand **what learners need to accomplish, what is preventing them from doing it, and how they prefer to learn**.

Some learners benefit from an **Action Mapping** approach: starting with what they need to **do**, identifying barriers to performance, and creating opportunities to **practice, receive feedback, and perform independently**.

## 🔄 Action Mapping

**Goal → Actions → Barriers → Practice → Essential Information**

## 🎯 Example: Learning by Doing

While working as a **Postdoctoral Research Scholar**, I met with **junior researchers** who wanted to learn how to conduct **meta-analyses in R**. I first met with them to understand **the problem they were experiencing, what they needed to accomplish, and how they preferred to learn**.

I learned that they preferred **learning by doing**. Rather than beginning with lectures or information-heavy instruction, I opened **RStudio and worked with them directly**.

We installed R and worked through the coding together. I demonstrated the procedures, explained coding best practices—such as writing comments as questions—and we solved problems as they came up. I then asked them to **perform the procedures independently** while I observed their work and provided feedback on how to approach and solve problems.

After they could work independently, they could return to me when they encountered a problem or wanted feedback.

Based on this experience, I created instructional videos demonstrating **R coding procedures** and explaining **meta-analysis concepts** so learners could follow along, **pause, replay, and revisit** the steps whenever they needed them.

The work grew into a **YouTube channel** covering R, meta-analysis, and academic writing, reaching more than **300 subscribers and 40,000 views**. Researchers from around the world, including **Germany and South Africa**, also contacted me with questions about the content.

### 🔍 How Action Mapping Shaped the Solution

- **Goal:** Researchers can independently perform R coding and meta-analysis procedures.
- **Actions:** Write R code and apply meta-analysis procedures to their own research.
- **Barriers:** Complex technical procedures can be difficult to perform independently.
- **Practice:** Code together, then perform the procedures independently.
- **Feedback:** Troubleshoot problems and provide guidance while learners practice.
- **Essential information:** Provide explanations and demonstrations directly connected to the task.

> **The solution focused on what researchers needed to do—not simply what they needed to know—and moved them from guided practice toward independent performance.**
